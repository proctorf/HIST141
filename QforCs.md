## Unit One: Towards Colonialism

<a id="topic-two"></a>
***Topic Two – Pre-Columbian Empires***

1) What observations can you make about Aztec and Inca society based on the primary source readings? How do they help us understand the lives of people living within those Empires?  
     
2) What were the primary “social markers” or differences that determined people’s status in Aztec, Inca, and Spanish societies prior to the Conquest?  Were they mainly economic, political, regional, or cultural?

3) How did religion play a role in the imperial domination by the Inca and the Aztecs?  In the Spanish encounter with and conquest of the New World?

4) Silverblatt refers to an Inca “imperial fantasy.”  What does she mean by this?  Did the Aztecs also engage in “imperial fantasy?”  How was it alike or different from that of the Inca?  How does the concept of “imperial fantasy” correspond to the model of state hegemony advanced by Roseberry, if at all?

***Topic Four – The Spiritual Conquest***

1) Discuss the multiple roles and influences of religion in the conquest and colonization of the New World by the Spanish?  Discuss the role of religion in establishing Spanish rule?  Did the crown, priests, and colonists share the same vision of colonial rule?

2) Compare and contrast Spanish with Aztec and Inca uses of religion in establishing and maintaining empire.  What would you identity as the principal difference between the two?

3) How successful were the Spaniards in converting the native populations of the New World?  Why?  Are you surprised?  Why might it be fair to call the early attempts at conversion the second or “spiritual” conquest?  What pre-Columbian patterns of belief and behavior, for both Spaniards and Indians, significantly impacted the success or failures of the conversion attempt from the perspective of the friars and the historian?

## Unit Two: Colonialism Entrenched

<a id="topic-five"></a>
***Topic Five - The Transition to Colonial Societies / Foundations of the Colonial Economy***

**Questions for Consideration:**  
 

1) How would you explain how the Spanish established their rule in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries from their perspective? Did the king, priests, encomenderos, and other members of Spanish society share the same vision of colonial rule?  
     
2) What about from the perspective of the natives?  Did native leaders and commoners have the same interests in accepting or resisting Spanish rule?  When did “acceptance” reach its limits, and what does that tell us about colonial hegemony?  
     
3) What was the Taki Oncoy?  How might it be related to our discussions of the spiritual conquest?  And, how does it reflect the ability or inability of the Spanish to impose a new imperial order on the Andes? Or, what can that tell us about colonial hegemony?  
     
4) Why might the Taki Oncoy and Viceroy Toledo’s reforms be seen as a “historical watershed”?  How was the nature of colonial rule changed following the Toledo’s reforms?  Did his reforms represent a revolution in government or the culmination of historical changes in governance?

<a id="topic-six"></a>
***Topic Six - Colonial Labor in Spanish America***

1) Which had the greatest effect on the operation of economy and tribute in Spanish America: royal policy and mercantilism; the importation of European models of production, pre-existing regional settlement and labor patterns among Indians, and/or the economic activities of colonial officials living in the colony?  What does your answer say about the nature of colonial rule; was it determined by the metropole or the colony?  Think about your answers in terms of mining, cochineal production, and the hacienda.

2) How did the nature of the “ayllu” change during the first 150 years of Spanish rule?  Do you see the survival of traditional Andean communities as a sign of the persistence of native culture, or had they become instruments of Spanish colonialism?  How would your answer compare to those for similar questions regarding the Indians of New Spain?  
     
3) How does a consideration of indigenous migration reshape your understandings of the changing nature of the ayllu?

## Unit Three – Colonial Society 

<a id="topic-seven"></a>
***Topic Seven – Brazil and Slavery***

1. Compare the life of the average male field slave and the life of the average urban female slave represented by Anastacía.  Be sure to consider what region of Brazil they might live in, in what kind of building they might sleep, what they might eat, what work the might perform, and what their relationships with other slaves and their masters might be.  Who had more a greater ability to alter their circumstances to their own benefit or at least to minimize disadvantage (that ability might be called agency)? 

2. How would you characterize the power differential in master-slave relationship?  Was the form and function of the institution of slavery imposed from above?  Did slaves have any control over their own destinies, and therefore over the form and function of slavery?  If so, what were there limits to that control?  How might issues like manumission, *roças*, and *quilombos* affect your answer?  And, how might the notion of hegemony help you frame your answer?

3. Are the experiences of slaves comparable to the indigenous workers we have discussed for Spanish America?  Did Brazilian slaves have similar opportunities to shape labor forms and socio-economic relations as Indians did in Peru and Mexico (if you even believe Indians had that ability)?  Were the results of those possibilities also potentially paradoxical for slaves?

***Topic Nine: Women, Gender, and Society***

1) How would you characterize colonial Latin American gender norms?  Were they entirely repressive for women?  Were they simply imposed from above?  Did women have the ability to maneuver within them? Could women escape those norms? Did women shape those norms? What about men, were they empowered or similarly constrained by colonial gender norms

2) How do the lives of Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz and Santa Rosa of Lima help us understand the paradoxical nature of gender norms and the honor-shame complex in colonial Latin America?

3) Should the colonial honor-shame culture be characterized as a rigid and repressive system that reinforced race, class and gender hierarchies? Or was it a more flexible culture that ultimately provided individuals the ability to break social norms and defy the hierarchies? 

4) How does looking at colonialism from the perspective of gender – rather than the Indian/Spanish dichotomy or master/slave categories – complicate questions of resistance and power?

## Unit Four – Colonial Collapse and Independence

<a id="topics-ten-eleven"></a>
***Topics Ten and Eleven: Bourbon Reforms and Independence***

1) To what degree where the Comunero Revolt, the Túpac Amaru uprising, and the Mexican rebellions caused by the Bourbon Reforms?  Did everyone participating in or fighting against these movements share the same goals?  How long did the alliances last and why?


2) Would you characterize the Comunero, Túpac Amaru, and the Mexican Rebellions as Revolutionary?  That is, did they seek to replace the colonial state with a new political order or only to change it?  How might Simon Bolivar and Miguel Hidalgo/José María Morelos be used to characterize the possibilities and results of the Independence movements?

3) Assess the degree to which each of the following was a cause of independence: a) the Bourbon Reforms; b) political events in late eighteenth and early nineteenth century Europe; c) alliances between creole elites and popular groups; and, d) changes in ideas about politics.  Consider what you answer says about the nature of top-down versus bottom up change in colonial society.


4) How might we explain the late eighteenth century rebellions and the independence movements in terms of hegemony if we see colonial stability as a result of “negotiated rule”?
