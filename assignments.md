## Grades and Grading:
Grading is a pedagogical tool. It is meant to signal to students where and how they can improve on meeting the expectations for the assignments in our class.  They are not a reflection of ability, character, or effort.  My comments on assignments are meant to highlight where improvement is possible on that assignment and should serve as guideposts for future assignments.  They are not a road map to earning “A’s.”  That may take time and practice.  And, I am here to help you in that journey.

I believe strongly in the power of constructive criticism, so my comments will focus on strengths I see in a particular assignment but perhaps more so on the areas that I see where improvement is possible.

Grading has become a more difficult task in the age of AI. It is harder and harder to distinguish between human and AI generated text. But, most importantly, what I care about is what YOU think and helping you hone your abilities to think critically and empathetically, and to communicate effectively.  

As a result, I am going to ask that you **DO NOT** use AI, other than spell/grammar check, to complete your written assignments.  And, to ensure that you are practicing the types of thinking I believe are important, grades will be assigned based on two components: 1\) the formal written assignment; and 2\) a **completely voluntary** oral discussion of your paper. My plan is to grade stringently, and if you are not entirely satisfied with the assigned grade, we will set up a 5-10 meeting to discuss your ideas. This will only serve to potentially increase your grade (you will not be penalized for taking this option).

## Course Requirements:

**Readings:**  There are three types of readings for this course.  In history, we distinguish between primary, secondary, and tertiary sources. Primary sources were written at or near the time under consideration.  Secondary sources are historical analyses written well after the event under consideration took place using primary sources. As a professional historian, I write secondary sources based on my reading of primary sources.  All of the primary and secondary sources for this course will be available under the “Readings” tab on the Canvas.  A textbook (or a tertiary source, one written to provide encyclopedic knowledge based on secondary materials), Mark Burkholder and Lyman Johnson’s *Colonial Latin America*, 10th ed., will be available on reserves in the library.     

Students are expected to have completed the assigned reading **prior** to the class period for which they are assigned.  Students ***must*** bring the readings to class; failure to do so will adversely affect your contribution grade.

**Informal Assignments** – Approximately once a week, you will be asked to complete a short informal assignment. Generally, this will take the form of an assignment on Canvas or an informal in-class writing assignment, in-class reading quiz, and/or a short online assignment after class.  The purpose of these assignments is not to “trick you” but rather to provide incentive to keep up with the work and to assess your learning throughout the semester.  More often than not, these will be short exercises available on Canvas. Please pay special attention to the notifications on Canvas and I will work to announce any such work in advance, both in-class and via email.  However, I reserve the right to assign in-class pop-quizzes should I deem it necessary.

These will be graded on a zero-credit, half-credit, three quarters credit, or full-credit basis (0, ✔, ✔\+, \+) based on the evidence of preparation, knowledge, and critical thinking you demonstrate in your responses.  Students will be allowed to drop the 2 (two) lowest scores on these assignments.

**Primary Source Analyses**: Your task is to analyze a primary source (or set of connected sources) in its/their historical context and to assess its/their value for your understanding of the past in a short (**750-word max, excluding notes**) paper.  You will complete all three such papers throughout the semester.

We will work together to learn exactly what this might mean throughout the semester with iterative assignments and some opportunity for peer review and revisions.

**Primary Source Analyses Due Dates**  
**Paper 1** (on primary source readings from before 9/15): **Due:** Tues 9/17 (revision due 9/22)  
**Paper 2** (on primary source readings from 9/22 \- 9/29) **Due**: Tues, Oct 6

Approach this assignment as if you are the first person to have discovered your primary source; and you are answering the question “why should a student of colonial Latin America read THIS source?”

You should try to express what it contains, what it means, and what its significance is to understanding the past.  You might think of this as aimed at a fellow historian (me and your classmates) who has not read the source in question.  

In addition, you should consider what the document *doesn’t say*: what did the author implicitly or explicitly omit, what can’t it tell us?  What sort of questions do the sources raise that can only be answered with other sources, primary or secondary?

Most importantly, you need to situate the document within the general questions of our class.  How does it help you articulate, and potentially answer, the questions most central to our studies?  

Your analysis should focus on the meaning and potential implications of the document in the context of colonial Latin America, and the author’s assumptions and arguments that form the basis of the document.  ***This is not meant to be a summary, but a presentation of arguments. An excellent summary with no interpretation can earn no higher than a C+.***

*A primary goal of history is to understand how people in the past saw themselves, their world(s), the issues of their time.  And, to do so in their context.  However, sometimes some of the things that we discuss will have modern resonance.  If you want to reflect on those contemporary issues, you MAY do so AFTER you complete the assignment above.  This is not required and will not impact my assessment of the assignment.*

***So, if you wish, you can write your 750-analysis, and then in a separate paragraph reflect on what the source and your interpretations of it might have to say about current realities.***

***Your grade on the assignment will focus solely and exclusively on your ability to make an argument about the past in your analysis.***

**Paper Format**: All papers must be double spaced, 12-point font, have proper footnotes (see the *Chicago Manual of Style, 18th ed*., for style guidelines), and be saved in Word (.doc) or GoogleDocs (.gdoc) format.  Papers saved in any other format will be considered late.

For a guide to the proper citation style, consult the section on the “Chicago Manual of Style, 18th edition” available at Purdue University’s “Online Writing Lab” at:  https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/chicago_manual_18th_edition/cmos_formatting_and_style_guide/chicago_manual_of_style_18th_edition.html

On each paper please include the following: Name, date, and Word Count in the upper left-hand corner followed by the title centered on the page (please do not include a title page).

**Contribution:** Every class period has an assigned reading and will include in-class discussion.  Students are expected to attend class, to have completed the reading, to bring the readings to class, to be prepared to discuss the major arguments of the work, and to contribute to our classroom conversations.

Denison, and History at Denison, are about the exchange of ideas.  Rather than thinking about contribution as something for you (an assignment), think of it as an obligation to your classmates.  They deserve for you to be prepared, engaged, and to share your ideas so that they can more thoroughly engage the material and the major questions we are considering.

**Late Papers:**  *Deadlines matter\!* This is an important thing to recognize. However, life happens and I want to be as supportive and flexible as I can relative to your ability to complete your assignments to the best of your ability and in a timely manner.  

*Proactivity also matters\!*  So, rather than a formal Late Policy, I am going to require that you be proactive.  If you contact me BEFORE a deadline to let me know that you need extra time (for whatever reason) AND provide a reasonable alternative deadline I will not impose late penalties.

However, if you are not proactive, and simply turn in an assignment late, I will impose late penalties as follows \- unexplained late papers will be penalized one full letter grade on the day following the due date (A to B) and one third of a letter grade (B to B-) for each day after that.  

Missed assignments will be assigned a score of zero.

Late informal assignments will not be accepted.
